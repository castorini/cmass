Abstract

One fixed corpus, four kinds of search task

Evaluating advances in agentic search requires testing whether methods generalize across tasks and corpus scale. Changing web search services impede controlled comparisons, while small corpora assembled around benchmark questions provide a limited test of generalization.

We introduce CMASS, the ClimbMix Agentic Search Suite, which projects three search benchmarks—BrowseComp-Plus, Total Recall QA, and DeepSearchQA—and the multi-hop question-answering dataset HotpotQA onto a shared, fixed corpus. The target, ClimbMix, is a pre-training corpus of 553M documents and approximately 400B tokens, assembled independently of the benchmark questions. Our projection pipeline verifies grounding, answer uniqueness, and set completeness through independent agent audits, controlled revision, and human review. The 423 questions span entity identification, multi-hop reasoning, exhaustive answer sets, and aggregation.

Evaluations of three models expose substantial headroom for open-weight agents and show that high answer accuracy can coexist with incomplete evidence coverage. By enabling measurement of accuracy alongside evidence coverage, search effort, and closed-book performance, CMASS supports developing agents that find sufficient evidence and produce complete answers efficiently.

The suite

Four benchmarks, projected onto ClimbMix

Each source question is decomposed into atomic reasoning hops and kept only if every hop is grounded in ClimbMix documents, its answer is unique and, for set questions, its answer set is complete. Questions that needed it were revised; wording and answers can differ from their upstream versions.

Every question comes with question-level relevance judgments (qrels): the ClimbMix documents that state a fact one of its hops needs, including exact and near duplicates of those documents.

Projection pipeline

Every hop grounded, every answer checked

  1. Preparation

    Exact and near-duplicate detection over the whole corpus (Jaccard 0.7 or more; nothing is removed), and one task per source question.

  2. Projection

    Agents decompose each question into hops, search ClimbMix for evidence of each hop, and judge whether the answer can be derived from what they found.

  3. Audit and review

    An auditor checks constraint coverage, uniqueness and set completeness and may revise the question; hunters look for rival answers; the authors review every question.

  4. Qrels

    Judge agents keep the pooled documents that support a hop and add their duplicates; documents the evaluated agents retrieved are then judged the same way, TREC-style.

Stage 2: audit, revision and human review

An auditor agent checks each question's constraints and hops (D2), then its answer's accuracy and completeness (D3). A question that fails goes to a revision agent for a permitted revision (D4); wording changes need human approval, and revised questions repeat the audit and review. Hunter and verifier agents look for rival answers and omissions, and the authors then accept, accept with fixes, or reject each question (D5). A fresh checker agent and validation scripts produce the final records (D6); HotpotQA skips the checker.

The authors' review interface, with an offline demo: review_site/.

The pipeline is dataset- and corpus-agnostic. Code, prompts and offline demos: pipelines/cmass.

Results

What three search agents show

  • Headroom for open-weight agents

    On BCP, Gemma 4 31B and Qwen 3.5 9B answer 27.5% and 16.3% of questions, against 85.0% for GPT-6.1 Sol, while making more tool calls.

  • Accuracy without full evidence

    Sol answers 90.1% of DSQA questions while grounding 75.7% of their hops. On HotpotQA it grounds 90.2% of hops from 17.2% of the relevant documents.

  • Strong closed-book baselines

    With no tools at all, Sol answers 89.7% of HotpotQA questions, against 94.6% with search. Closed-book runs check whether accuracy gains also occur without retrieval.
